# When Innovation Fails: Whatever Happened to CurrentC?

**Source:** Enrique Dans, Medium, 2018-09-11
**URL:** https://medium.com/enrique-dans/when-innovation-fails-whatever-happened-to-currentc-b7f40c8bf39b

## Why this matters to agentic payments

CurrentC is the canonical case study of what happens when a payment system solves the **merchant's problem** but not the **consumer's problem** — a structural failure pattern that applies directly to evaluating agentic commerce protocols.

## What was CurrentC

CurrentC (2014–2016) was the MCX (Merchant Customer Exchange) consortium's QR-code payment system, backed by Walmart, Target, Best Buy, CVS, Rite Aid, Kmart, 7-Eleven, etc. They actively blocked Apple Pay (NFC) at their terminals to prevent Apple from taking a slice of payments.

**The consumer value proposition:** Scan a QR code instead of tapping your phone. Funds debited directly from bank account.

**The merchant value proposition:** Eliminate ~2-3% credit card interchange. Keep loyalty card data. Maintain customer relationship.

## Why it failed

1. **Solved the wrong problem:** Eliminated interchange for merchants while offering no advantage — and an actual downside — to consumers vs. the simpler Apple Pay
2. **Security breach early on:** Hacked during testing, discrediting it
3. **Coalition friction:** Merchants individually started defecting (Best Buy 2015, Rite Aid 2015)
4. **Consumer backlash:** App reviews tank; customers angry about NFC being blocked
5. **Terminated May 2016** — June 28 was the last day, all accounts deactivated

## The lesson for agentic commerce

The CurrentC pattern is: incumbent players (here, large merchants) build a competing system to protect their economics, but fail to make the user experience better. The new system captures no consumer adoption because it doesn't solve a consumer pain point.

This is the exact pattern that **Walmart's Sparky experiment** ran on deliberately. Walmart tested both agent-owned checkout (ACP/ChatGPT Instant Checkout) and merchant-owned checkout (Sparky). The result — merchant-owned won 2:1 on conversion — validates that agentic commerce, like mobile payments, only works if the consumer experience is better, not just the merchant economics.

> "The whole thing turned out to be a text-book failure, one whose limitations were discussed at the time, but which were ignored by those behind it."

The parallel to agentic payments: any protocol that solves the infrastructure/economics problem for fintechs but creates friction for end users (human or agent) will fail.
