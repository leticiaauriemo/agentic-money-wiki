# Mercury

**One-line:** Startup-focused neobank becoming an actual chartered bank; raised $200M at $5.2B valuation and received OCC conditional approval in April 2026.

## What they're building

Mercury is transitioning from a bank-partner-model neobank to a federally regulated bank in its own right. Having served startups and growth companies since 2019, it filed with the OCC in December 2025 and received conditional approval by April 2026 — with final approval expected in 2027.

Once chartered: Mercury will hold deposits directly (currently held at Column and Choice Financial), join Zelle, expand into lending, and stop sharing revenue with partner banks. A $650M revenue fintech and a $650M revenue bank are wildly different businesses in terms of margin.

## Relevance to agentic money

Mercury is primarily relevant as the "bank for AI-native businesses" — seeing 2.5x more applications in Q1 2026 vs Q1 2025, driven by the explosion of AI-founded companies. Stripe Atlas signups are spiking for the same reason: AI lets people build more with less, creating a new generation of founders who need a bank account from day one. Mercury benefits from being the default choice for these companies.

If Mercury becomes chartered and direct-to-Fed, it could also become a more direct infrastructure player for agent-initiated payments — holding agent wallets, providing API-native banking without partner bank dependencies.

## Funding & traction

- **Series D (2026-05):** $200M at $5.2B valuation (49% jump from last round), led by TCV; investors include Sequoia, Andreessen Horowitz, and Coatue
- **OCC conditional approval:** April 2026, filed December 2025; final approval expected 2027
- **AI-driven growth:** 2.5x more applications Q1 2026 vs Q1 2025
