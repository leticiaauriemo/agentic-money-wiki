# Agentic AI Payments: Navigating Consumer Protection, Innovation, and Regulatory Frameworks

**Source:** [Consumer Bankers Association (CBA)](https://consumerbankers.com/research/agentic-ai-payments-navigating-consumer-protection-innovation-and-regulatory-frameworks/)
**Publisher:** Consumer Bankers Association
**Published:** January 2026 (based on Fall 2025 symposium)

## Four critical findings

### 1. EFTA uncertainty
The Electronic Fund Transfer Act (1978) has uncertain application to agentic payments. If an agent makes an unauthorized or erroneous transfer, existing law may make the **consumer liable** rather than the financial institution or agent developer. This is the most significant consumer protection gap.

### 2. New rails = fewer protections
Stablecoins and crypto platforms may emerge with fewer consumer protections than traditional card networks. Card networks voluntarily exceed federal requirements:
- Zero liability for unauthorized transactions
- Chargeback rights
- Dispute resolution processes

Emerging crypto/AI rails have no equivalent guarantees.

### 3. Banks as escalation points
When agents make mistakes, consumers will contact their bank. Banks must prepare for escalating inquiry volumes even for agent-initiated transactions that didn't originate with the bank.

### 4. Regulatory change unlikely near-term
Under the current Trump administration, immediate regulatory change is unlikely. CBA recommends **industry self-regulation** through private network rules across the full ecosystem.

## CBA's recommendation

Develop private network rules covering:
- Agent developers
- Merchant platforms
- Banks and financial institutions
- Issuing platforms

These rules should establish clear liability allocation and dispute resolution standards before regulation catches up.

## Significance

This is the banking industry's own trade association identifying consumer liability as the central unresolved policy question. Unlike tech-optimist framings, CBA focuses on what happens when agents make mistakes.
