# Earning Consumer Trust in the Age of Agentic Commerce — Visa Research

**Source:** Visa Research (PDF report)
**Markets:** US (n=1,600), Australia (n=1,600), New Zealand (n=500)

## Familiarity and adoption

- **US:** 55% familiar with AI shopping assistants
- **Australia:** 59% familiar (highest of three markets)
- **New Zealand:** 43% familiar
- ~**1 in 3 consumers** across all markets expect to use AI agents regularly

## Buyer journey delegation rates

How willing consumers are to let agents handle each stage of shopping:

| Stage | Willingness to delegate |
|-------|------------------------|
| Discovery | 73% |
| Evaluation | 69% |
| Cart & checkout | 62% |
| Post-purchase | 64% |

## What motivates adoption

- Save time: 42%
- Save money: 41%
- Convenience: 32%

## What blocks adoption

- Data privacy/security concerns: 50%
- Privacy generally: 44%
- Purchase accuracy fears: 42%
- Reliability: 40%
- Loss of control: 36%

## Critical control stat

- **85%** say data visibility and customization is important
- **54% would stop using agents if they lost data control**

## Most trusted brands for AI agents

**Tier 1 (~42% trust each):** PayPal, Amazon, Visa
**Tier 2 (~28% trust each):** Apple, Google, Mastercard
**Tier 3:** Microsoft, Meta, American Express

## Decision factors when shopping online

1. Price (77%)
2. Delivery (66%)
3. Ease of use (58%)

## Business opportunity

**1 in 3 consumers** more likely to shop with retailers offering agentic options — signal of early-mover ROI.

**McKinsey projection:** Agentic commerce reaching **$3–5T globally by 2030**; **$1T in US alone**.

**1 in 6** Black Friday 2025 purchases involved AI-assisted buying.

## Key findings for ecosystem design

1. Consistency across US, AU, NZ suggests universal consumer expectations — not a market-specific phenomenon
2. Tokenization and data transparency are non-negotiable for consumer loyalty
3. Hybrid experiences (human + AI) help build early confidence
4. Merchants must optimize product data for machine-readability (not just human SEO)
5. Financial institutions that become the **permissioning and control layer** win; those relegated to invisible funding sources lose

## Relevance to agentic money

This is the most rigorous consumer research in the wiki. The 54% loss-of-control abandonment stat and the trust hierarchy (PayPal > Amazon > Visa >> Apple > Google) have direct implications for who controls the agent commerce layer.
