# The 2028 Global Intelligence Crisis (Citrini Scenario)

**Source:** CitriniResearch / Citrini, written 2026-02-22, set in June 2028
**URL:** https://www.citriniresearch.com/p/2028gic

## What this is

A scenario analysis ("thought exercise in financial history from the future") exploring what happens if AI-driven productivity growth is real but the gains bypass households — causing a consumer spending collapse, private credit defaults, and mortgage stress. Explicitly framed as a tail-risk scenario, not a prediction.

## Key scenario elements

### Phase 1: 2026 — The productivity boom masks the problem
- S&P 500 at 8000, Nasdaq above 30k by October 2026
- White-collar layoffs begin, margins expand, stocks rally
- "Ghost GDP" — output that shows up in national accounts but doesn't circulate through the real economy
- Top 10% of earners drive 50%+ of consumer spending; they are the first to lose jobs

### Phase 2: Agentic commerce disrupts intermediation (2026-2027)
- Qwen's open-source agentic shopper catalyzes AI handling consumer decisions across all assistants
- By March 2027, median US individual consumes 400,000 tokens/day (10x since end of 2026)
- Agents remove friction → subscriptions, teaser rates, and passive renewals get optimized away
- Travel, insurance, real estate, financial advice disrupted by agents with perfect information
- **The payment rails shift:** Agents routing around 2-3% card interchange → stablecoins on Solana/Ethereum L2s
- Mastercard Q1 2027: purchase volume growth slows to +3.4% YoY; management notes "agent-led price optimization"

### Phase 3: Private credit crisis (2027)
- PE-backed software SaaS LBOs fail as AI destroys ARR assumptions
- Zendesk default: $5B direct lending facility marked to 58 cents (largest private credit software default)
- Insurance subsidiaries of PE firms (Athene/Apollo, Global Atlantic/KKR) face capital pressure
- "Permanent capital" proves not-so-permanent when underlying loans default

### Phase 4: Mortgage question (2028)
- Prime borrowers (780 FICO) with tech/finance jobs hit income impairment
- Zillow: -11% YoY in SF, -9% Seattle, -8% Austin
- Fannie Mae flags elevated delinquencies in high-tech-employment zip codes

## Relevance to agentic payments

The scenario contains a specific payments prediction: once agents control transactions, they "went looking for bigger paperclips" — specifically **targeting the 2-3% interchange fee** as the biggest available savings. Agents choose stablecoins on L2s (sub-cent cost) over cards.

> "In machine-to-machine commerce, the 2-3% card interchange rate became an obvious target."

This is the bear case for Mastercard/Visa that underpins the stablecoin-first thesis: AI agents are programmed to minimize costs and will route around interchange once alternatives exist.

## Counterpoint embedded in the scenario

The scenario explicitly notes Visa was better positioned than Mastercard ("Visa pared losses after analysts pointed out its stronger positioning in stablecoin infrastructure") — because Visa's tokenization and stablecoin settlement programs give it a hedge.

## Open questions this raises

- At what transaction scale do agents actually start routing around interchange? (x402 average ticket: $0.31)
- Can card networks adapt fast enough via Agent Pay / Intelligent Commerce?
- Does interchange pressure come from stablecoins, or from card tokenization creating agent-native credentials?
