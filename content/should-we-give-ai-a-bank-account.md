# Should We Give AI a Bank Account?

**Source:** [Possible with Reid Hoffman and Aria Finger, 2026-03-31](https://www.possible.fm/podcasts/neville/)
**Guest:** Sean Neville (co-founder Circle, founder [[catena-labs]])

## Key arguments

### Every financial transaction will eventually be executed by AI

Sean's core belief: the window to get agentic finance right is closing due to rapid AI acceleration. The question isn't whether agents will handle money — it's whether infrastructure is ready.

### Why stablecoins beat credit cards for agent payments

Credit cards fail for agents because they lack:
- Agent identity verification
- Spending rules enforceable in code
- Full auditability
- Programmable enforcement at the ledger level

Stablecoins solve all four: smart contracts can enforce spending limits, transaction velocity throttling, and spending categories — all in code, not human oversight.

### Guardrails require two layers

1. **AI layer** — prompt-based suggestions (insufficient alone; see [[ramp]] research on why agents ignore soft constraints)
2. **Deterministic enforcement layer** — blockchain/smart contracts; the hard constraint that agents cannot override

This is why stablecoins are the right currency for agents, not credit cards.

### Know Your Agent is the critical unsolved problem

KYA must work as well as KYC. In the near term, agent identity maps back to a licensed financial institution. In the future, agents may have their own independent identities. [[catena-labs]] is building for both scenarios.

### The "singleness of money" problem

A world with 10,000 stablecoins fails because a dollar must always equal a dollar. USDC's liquidity moat (not its technology) is what makes it the default. Creating a stablecoin is easy; achieving meaningful liquidity is not.

### OpenClaw data point

145,000+ GitHub stars in two weeks; 1.5 million agents transacting on multiple platforms — but also showed security/malware risks, underlining the need for guardrails.
